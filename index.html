01 — THE APPROACH

Two measurements.
One canopy.

Leaves interact with light. We bring together a measurement made on sampled foliage and a laser measurement of its surroundings, then ask whether they vary together.

ON THE FOLIAGE

A field chlorophyll signal

A CCM-300 instrument measures the ratio of fluorescence at 735 and 700 nm. This chlorophyll fluorescence ratio, or CFR, is our field comparison.

735 nm/700 nmFLUORESCENCE

CFR is a measured optical signal. Converting it to chlorophyll concentration requires separate validation, especially for conifers.

FROM THE LASER

A two-colour reflectance ratio

The LiDAR records reflected laser light in two channels. Within each sample cube, we divide the mean calibrated reflectance at 1064 nm by the mean at 532 nm.

1064 nm/532 nmMEAN REFLECTANCE

These are channel averages from nearby returns, not two measurements of the exact same leaf or a point-by-point ratio.

What does “within-date” mean?

We subtract each date’s mean from both signals, then correlate those residuals. This describes association after removing date averages. It is not the average of the per-date correlations or a validated prediction score.

04 — WHAT WE CAN SAY

Promising patterns.
Open questions.

These results describe the current observations. They are a starting point for testing how reliably laser measurements can represent chlorophyll across different canopies.

01

Season matters

Beech’s pooled correlation is 0.913, but its within-date value is 0.597. A strong seasonal pattern does not establish accurate estimates for every location on every date.

02

Cleaning needs evidence

At Beech, removing both structures and predicted wood changes pooled r from 0.905 to 0.913. The uncertainty range for that change spans zero; an improvement is not established.

03

Validation continues

Independent checks of full-site alignment and material labels remain open. Model-predicted leaves, unmarked returns and fluorescence ratios each need careful interpretation.

HOW TO READ THIS SNAPSHOT

We use supported exact-date observations from 12 March to 18 June 2026. Beech contributes five dates; Douglas and Mixed contribute six. The 20 March flight and 26 May field readings are excluded under the current analysis policy. Missing observations stay missing.

All four cleaning choices compare the same supported locations and dates within each tower. Reflectance must be finite and within 0–100, scanner distance within 15–50 m, with at least five accepted returns in each channel.

Methods, source data and reproducibility

This is the 13 October 2026 exact-date analysis: 109 pairs across 23 locations, evaluated in four cleaning states. The source cohort contains 117 candidate exact-date pairs; 109 have common support across all states. The March24/26 date-tolerance sensitivity is separate and is not shown here.

Analysis positions use reviewed September GNSS observations, with the approved May FIX observation for Douglas dt24wg. Structure selection uses the dated geometry-only default; wood labels come from existing PointsToWood predictions. Sample support and position uncertainty are not eliminated by cleaning.

The source research is maintained in the private LeafH2O repository. The public snapshot includes the rows behind this page, summary coefficients, source hashes, and a real-point display sample. No site statistic is calculated from that display sample.
